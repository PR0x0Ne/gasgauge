## 📊 Understanding the Numbers

You do not need to understand the inner workings of Ethereum to use gasgauge, but three terms will help you read the screen with confidence.

**Gas** is a unit of work. Sending a simple payment uses a small, fixed amount of gas. Interacting with a more complex application uses more. gasgauge estimates based on a typical simple transaction, which is what most people are doing.

**Gwei** is the unit Ethereum uses to price gas. It is a very small fraction of a single coin. When the app shows a number in gwei, that is the per-unit price of gas at that moment. Higher gwei means a more expensive transaction and, usually, a faster one.

**Base fee** is the minimum the network charges, set automatically by the protocol. It rises when the network is busy and falls when it is quiet. gasgauge separates the base fee from the optional tip so you can see exactly where your money is going.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting

**The window opens but shows no numbers.** Check your internet connection, then close and reopen the app. Corporate networks and some VPNs can block the data feed.

**Windows will not let me run the file.** Click **More info** on the security panel, then **Run anyway**. If your organisation manages the computer, you may need permission from your IT team.

**I am not getting alerts.** Make sure notifications are enabled for gasgauge in Windows Settings under System → Notifications, and confirm the alert is switched on in the app.

**The numbers look frozen.** The status bar at the bottom shows the last refresh time. If it is more than a few minutes old, the connection has dropped.

## 📝 Glossary

- **Blockchain** — the public record of transactions that Ethereum runs on.
- **ETH** — the currency of the Ethereum network.
- **Gas** — the unit that measures how much work a transaction requires.
- **Gwei** — the small unit used to price gas.
- **EIP-1559** — the Ethereum fee system that split fees into a base fee plus a tip, which gasgauge displays separately.
- **DeFi** — decentralised finance, a category of applications where transaction fees matter a lot.
- **Transaction cost** — the total fee you pay to move funds or interact with an application.
